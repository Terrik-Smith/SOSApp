# SOS Connect — Brand Identity Guide

## 1. Purpose

This document is the visual and verbal source of truth for SOS Connect.

Everything built for SOS Connect should follow the standards defined in this guide.

This includes:

- Website design
- Application interfaces
- Buttons
- Forms
- Typography
- Colors
- Marketing materials
- Brand messaging
- Future product screens

---

# 2. Logo

SOS Connect uses the existing official SOS Connect logo.

### Primary Logo

The existing SOS Connect logo should be used as the primary brand mark.

Current project path:

`/images/sos-connect-logo.png`

### Logo Rules

- Preserve the original proportions.
- Do not stretch or distort the logo.
- Do not modify the logo's design.
- Do not add effects.
- Do not replace the logo with generated artwork.
- Maintain appropriate space around the logo.
- Use the official logo consistently across the application.

### Logo Assets

The project should maintain official logo assets in:

`brand/assets/`

Preferred formats:

- PNG
- SVG

The SVG should only be added when an official or accurately converted version of the existing logo is available.

### SOS Navy

Represents:

- Trust
- Stability
- Confidence
- Safety

### SOS Red

Represents:

- Action
- Connection
- Urgency when appropriate
- The primary SOS Connect call to action

### White

Represents:

- Clarity
- Simplicity
- Accessibility
- Clean design

---

# 4. Color Usage Rules

### Primary Action

The primary CONNECT action should use:

**SOS Red `#ED2635`**

with white text.

### Primary Brand Background

Major brand sections may use:

**SOS Navy `#06182A`**

with white text.

### Hover / Active State

Use:

**SOS Red Dark `#D91F2D`**

for appropriate hover or pressed states.

### Light Sections

Use:

**Soft Gray `#F5F7F9`**

to separate sections without overwhelming the interface.

### Accessibility

Text should maintain strong contrast against its background.

Do not use SOS Red as a large body-text color on white backgrounds when readability would be reduced.

# 5. Typography

SOS Connect uses three primary typefaces.

## Display / Headings

### Fraunces

Use Fraunces for:

- Hero headlines
- Major headings
- Brand statements
- Emotional or editorial moments
- Large display text

Example:

**Help Is Closer Than You Think.**

---

## Body / Interface

### Public Sans

Use Public Sans for:

- Body text
- Navigation
- Buttons
- Forms
- Instructions
- Descriptions
- Labels
- Interface elements

Public Sans should provide the primary readable interface language.

---

## Numbers / Metadata

### IBM Plex Mono

Use IBM Plex Mono for:

- Distance
- Miles
- Status information
- Timestamps
- Statistics
- Small metadata

Example:

`2.4 MI AWAY`

---

# 7. Buttons

## Primary Action

The primary SOS Connect action is:

# CONNECT

Primary CONNECT buttons should use:

- SOS Red `#ED2635`
- White text
- Clear typography
- Strong visual hierarchy

The homepage may use the large circular CONNECT button as the primary action.

### CONNECT Purpose

CONNECT takes the member into the help-request experience.

Current request page:

`/request-help.html`

---

## Secondary Action

The primary secondary action is:

**Become a Connector**

This allows people who want to provide help to enter the Connector experience.

---

# 8. Navigation

Navigation should remain simple and easy to understand.

Preferred language includes:

- Home
- Get Help
- Become a Connector
- About

Avoid overly technical navigation terminology.

---

# 9. Homepage Visual Hierarchy

The homepage should communicate the SOS Connect concept quickly.

The hierarchy should generally be:

1. SOS Connect logo
2. Tagline
3. CONNECT action
4. Need Help / Become a Connector
5. Six assistance categories
6. How It Works
7. Nearby / community information
8. Footer

The CONNECT action should remain the primary call to action.

---

# 10. Six Core Categories

The homepage displays six informational categories:

1. Vehicle Assistance
2. Home & Yard
3. Transportation
4. Care & Support
5. Community Sharing
6. Emergency & Recovery

These categories are informational on the homepage.

They should not compete with the primary CONNECT action.

Detailed service selection happens inside the help-request experience.

# 11. Tone and Voice

SOS Connect should sound like a person helping another person.

## Voice Pillar 1 — Friendly

Use approachable language.

Example:

**Need a hand?**

Avoid:

**Submit assistance request.**

---

## Voice Pillar 2 — Reassuring

Help people feel comfortable asking for support.

Example:

**You're not alone.**

Avoid language that makes someone feel embarrassed or judged for needing help.

---

## Voice Pillar 3 — Clear

Use simple language that explains what happens next.

Example:

**Tell us what you need.**

Avoid:

**Initiate a community assistance service request.**

---

## Voice Pillar 4 — Respectful

Treat every person with dignity.

Example:

**Let's find someone nearby who can help.**

---

## Voice Pillar 5 — Confident

SOS Connect should communicate that the platform is capable and dependable.

Example:

**Help is closer than you think.**

---

# 12. Verbal System

SOS Connect is organized around three ideas:

## NEED

**Need a hand?**

## CONNECT

**Let's find someone nearby.**

## GIVE

**Become a Connector.**

Together:

**Need → Connect → Give**

---

# 13. Tagline

## Help Is Closer Than You Think.

The tagline represents the central promise of SOS Connect:

People who need help may have someone nearby who is willing and able to provide it.

---

# 14. Big Brand Idea

# Help Goes Both Ways.

SOS Connect is designed around a two-sided community.

One person may need help today.

That same person may be able to help someone else tomorrow.

The platform should communicate that receiving help and giving help are both valuable parts of the community.

---

# 15. Design Principles

Every SOS Connect interface should follow these principles:

### Simple

Do not overwhelm people with unnecessary information.

### Human

Design for people first, not technology first.

### Clear

Users should understand what to do next.

### Trustworthy

The interface should feel safe and professional.

### Accessible

People with different needs and circumstances should be able to use the platform.

### Community-Oriented

The design should reinforce connection between people.
